Research

Reliable AI from the inside out

diagnose the mechanism → repair the failure → deploy with trust

I study how large multimodal models and agents compute internally, and why they fail. I use that understanding to build models that reason reliably, in training and at inference, and agents that act reliably in open, embodied environments.

View publications Google Scholar

01 · Diagnose

Mechanistic Diagnosis of Multimodal Models

Reliability starts with knowing where things break. I analyze information flow, attention, and saliency inside vision–language models and diffusion MLLMs to explain failures such as hallucination, attention sinks, and the repetition curse under cache acceleration.

  • information flow
  • attention sinks
  • hallucination
  • dMLLMs

02 · Repair

Reliable Reasoning, in Training and Inference

Each diagnosis becomes a principled fix. When models learn, I redesign positional encodings and attention structure so they keep visual and spatial grounding. When they generate, I design decoding and token-level strategies that keep outputs coherent and faithful.

  • training-time priors
  • inference-time intervention
  • positional encoding
  • decoding

03 · Deploy

Reliable Agents in Open Environments

Agents that improve themselves also need to be checked. I study when the skills a self-evolving agent accumulates actually help, exposing failures such as coalition pollution and cross-domain utility reversal. I also study how embodied models can interleave reasoning with action.

  • self-evolution
  • skill reliability
  • embodied reasoning
